    These are the CLI's optional arguments, named as in its usage text with their position in brackets, at their CLI defaults. The two left out are fixed here: --full_sam [#14] is always off and --out_fmt [#15] always SAM, so the numbers skip from #13 to #16. Leave them alone unless you have a reason to change them.
    --large_del_len [#22]. A deletion this long or longer inside one read is a large deletion; shorter ones are small indels. Default 50; use 100 for the paper's large-deletion definition.
    --dist_cut [#13]. How far a large deletion's read ends may be from the cut, which caps the deletion size that can be called. Default 8000; at 1000 most deletions over 3 kb are lost. Small indels are not affected.
    --common_mode [#24]. Pair-based: a treated deletion is common (an artefact) when at least 4 control pairs have both breakpoints within max(20 bp, 2% of its length) and their frequency is at least half the treated one; the result does not depend on read order. k-means compares cluster centres and can vary between runs for deletions over 2 kb.
    --min_gap [#16]. Smallest gap between mates for a pair without a split read to count as a large deletion. An insert-size floor, not the 100 bp definition.
    --anchor_min [#17]. Lowest split-read anchor score (length − 4×mismatches). 0 turns it off; 16 is strict.
    --ins_max [#20]. Longest insertion still recorded as one.
    With ins_fix off, most insertions of 9 bp or more are lost. Clip rescue re-calls a small indel within 13 bp of a read end, seen only as a soft clip, as the same allele seen with full anchors in at least 3 pairs of the sample; with it off, about 10% of 1–10 bp deletions count as terminal insertions.
